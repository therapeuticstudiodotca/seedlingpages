Weaving

Over and under

The box goes into the ground. The strip stays with you. After a few pages you have a few, and they weave into a small square of colour with no picture in it.

1When you are ready, weave your strips, over and under.

How to weave

Wait until you have at least four strips. Lay half of them side by side on the table, touching, running away from you. Take another strip and pass it across them: over the first, under the second, over the third. The next strip starts the other way, under and then over. Push each one up snug against the last.

Each strip is about two and a half inches wide and as long as the page is wide, so three going one way and three the other make a square of about seven and a half inches. If you’d like a finer weave, cut a strip in half along its length. A dab of glue or a stitch at each corner holds the square, or you can leave it loose and add to it as more pages come.

There is no pattern to follow. Colours you chose on different days end up beside each other and cross. The lines from one plant run into the lines from another. Nothing in the square is a picture, and nothing in it needs to be.

The workbook ends with an empty frame for your weaving, if you’d like somewhere to keep it.
